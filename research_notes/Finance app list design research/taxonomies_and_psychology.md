# Category taxonomies and budgeting mental models (apps + behavioural research)

Scope: default top-level spending groups in leading budgeting apps and methods, exact definitions and edge-case placement, what behavioural research says about category count / discretionary buckets / fixed-vs-variable framing, and how apps handle remapping and group- vs category-level targets. App facts are as of 2025–2026 unless marked legacy. Research date: 2026-11-09.

Access notes: `help.monarch.com` and `help.copilot.money` partly returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so some Monarch/Copilot facts come from Monarch's own blog (monarch.com), Copilot's intercom mirror, or search snippets of the help pages (marked "snippet"). Three PDFs (CFPB 2017, Alenazi & Sas 2023, Fei/Bartels/Zhang 2024) were read in full via local text extraction.

## Key Question 1 — Default top-level groups per app / method (the lists)

### Takeaway
Apps converge on roughly 10–20 default spending categories and 3–6 structural "types" (Income, Expense, Transfer, plus Excluded/Ignored). Only two products ship a *behavioural* top layer by default: Monarch (Fixed / Flexible / Non-monthly + Goals, "flex budgeting" is the default) and YNAB (Bills / Frequent / Non-Monthly / Goals / Quality of Life Goals). Everything else ships a flat, merchant-style taxonomy (Auto & Transport, Bills & Utilities, Dining, Groceries, Shopping, Entertainment, Travel, Health, Gifts, Fees…) and leaves the needs/wants/fixed/flex layer to the user.

### Cited Findings

**YNAB (default category groups)**
- Default groups for a new plan: "Bills", "Frequent", "Non-Monthly", "Goals", "Quality of Life Goals". Bills = "Categories for your monthly expenses"; Frequent = groceries, transportation, eating out; Non-Monthly "breaks True Expenses, such as holiday gifts, annual subscriptions, or car insurance premiums, into monthly amounts"; Goals = things you're saving up for (travel, education, home improvements); Quality of Life Goals = hobbies, health and wellness, entertainment. — [YNAB blog: How many YNAB categories](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-many-ynab-categories)
- YNAB's own advice: the right number "is different for everyone"; "budgeting beginners start with fewer categories"; let new categories "earn their place"; ask whether a category will change your behaviour. — [YNAB blog](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-many-ynab-categories)
- "True Expenses" (= sinking funds) are "money you gradually set aside to cover specific non-monthly expenses" — examples: car repairs, insurance premiums, holiday gifts, home repairs, tuition, annual membership; also "likely but uncertain" items (car breakdown, doctor, laptop dies) and planned discretionary (Christmas, vacation, summer camp). Set up "like you would any other category" with a savings target. — [YNAB blog: True Expenses](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-to-embrace-your-true-expenses)
- A 2018 third-party tutorial lists the then-default "True Expenses" categories as Auto maintenance, Home Maintenance, Renter's/Home Insurance, Medical, Clothing, Gifts, Giving, Computer Replacement, Software Subscriptions (dated; current defaults differ in group naming). — [DevelopGoodHabits YNAB tutorial PDF, 2018](https://www.developgoodhabits.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/YNAB-Tutorial.pdf)
- Inflows into checking accounts auto-categorise to "Income: Ready to Assign". Payee default category = most recently used; it flips when "2 of the 3 most recent transactions use a different category"; "Always use my choice" locks it. — [YNAB support: Categorizing transactions](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/categorizing-transactions-a-guide-HyRl60sks.md)

**Monarch**
- Monarch "sorts spending in three layers: type, group, and category" and "starts you with about 60 default categories" you can "turn off, rename, or add to"; first budget "fills in with your last six months' average spending". — [Monarch blog: 23 budget categories](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget)
- Default categories include a "Transfers" group; default categories "can be disabled but not deleted"; named defaults include "Gifts & Donations" and "Auto & Transport" (snippet of help article). — [Monarch help: Default Categories](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048883851)
- Flex budgeting is the default: "you will group your expenses into Fixed, Non-monthly, or Flex buckets" (snippet). You can move money to/from Fixed and Non-monthly categories and the overall Flex bucket, "but not the individual categories inside Flex" (snippet). — [Monarch help: Creating Your Budget](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048883631-Creating-Your-Budget-in-Monarch)
- Monarch's default is flex; switchable to category budgeting in budget settings. Flex = "big picture" with three buckets Fixed / Flexible / Non-monthly; Category = "detailed limits for individual category types". — [Monarch: Flex vs category budgeting](https://www.monarch.com/flex-vs-category-budgeting-how-to-choose-whats-right-for-you)
- Monarch's recommended 23-category list (blog, not the app default): Fixed (9): House expenses; Childcare or pet care; Regular self care; Utilities; Phone, internet, cable; Cars; Debts; Memberships and subscriptions; Monthly donations. Flexible (6): Groceries; Out-of-pocket healthcare; Transportation; Dining out, entertainment, hobbies; Grooming; Miscellaneous. Non-monthly (8 stated, 9 listed): Gifts and entertaining; Travel and vacations; Home and car maintenance; Property taxes; Tuition (semi/annual); Summer camp; Clothing or home accessories; Insurance premiums; Quarterly/annual taxes. Goals (separate): Emergency Fund; Retirement Savings; Other Savings; Investments; Debt Repayment – Credit Cards / Student Loans / Other Loans. — [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget)

**Copilot Money**
- Ships "a set of default spending categories during the onboarding process"; new categories are "Regular" (counted) or "Excluded" ("not included in your spending and budget calculations") (snippet). — [Copilot help: Creating new categories](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/3763839-creating-new-categories)
- "Other" is the only undeletable category (renameable); it catches transactions with no aggregator category and no rule; "We do not support categorizing internal transfer transactions"; transfers "don't count toward your monthly spend"; "Income categories aren't supported" (use Tags). — [Copilot help: Categories FAQ](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/10216528-categories-faq)
- Groups are user-created ("Food" with Groceries + Restaurants is the example); no predefined group names. — [Copilot help: Groups of categories](https://intercom.help/copilotmoney/en/articles/3767655-groups-of-categories)
- Initial budget "based on your historic spend"; "We do not correlate your total budget with your income"; Recurrings (bills, subscriptions, reimbursements) are tracked separately and excluded from the dashboard chart. — [Copilot quick start](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/11157550-quick-start-guide)
- Copilot's budget framework described as "category limits with optional rollovers", not zero-based. — [envelopebudgeting.com review](https://envelopebudgeting.com/articles/copilot-money-review)

**Rocket Money**
- Transactions are auto-sorted "into our predefined categories"; custom categories are Premium-only; each custom category gets a category group of "Expenses, Earnings, or Ignored" and a flag whether it counts toward spend or income. — [Rocket Money help: Editing/creating categories](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/3332081-reviewing-editing-and-creating-transaction-categories)
- Five "non-spending categories" ("money movements that don't actually involve you spending money right now"): Tax-Deductible; Reimbursements ("receiving money or getting paid for money that you've spent"); Ignored; Transfers ("moving money from one account to the other"); Income. — [Rocket Money help: Non-spending categories](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/10770105-understanding-non-spending-categories-in-your-budget)
- Rocket Money's editorial "8 budget categories" (with suggested shares): Bills And Utilities 25–35% (sub: Housing, Utilities); Auto And Transport 10–15%; Groceries 10–15% (sub: Home And Garden, Pets); Medical 5–10%; Health And Wellness 5–10% (sub: Personal Care, Family Care); Loan Payment 10–20%; Dining And Drinks 5–10%; Recreation And Entertainment 5–10% (sub: Shopping, Travel And Vacation). These mirror the app's category names seen in reviews ("Auto & Transport", "Dining & Drinks", "Family Care"). — [Rocket Money Learn: Budget categories](https://rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/budget-categories); [College Investor review](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/22660/rocket-money-review/)
- Users have requested "fixed versus discretionary segments and nested subcategories". — [Rocket Money Canny: Subcategories](https://rocketmoney.canny.io/feature-requests/p/subcategories)

**Mint (legacy, shut down)**
- Mint's built-in categories could not be deleted; user subcategories "must belong to one of the main categories listed in Mint". — [Mint help (archived page)](https://help.mint.com/Accounts-and-Transactions/888960541/How-do-I-add-a-transaction-subcategory.htm)
- Third-party userscript existed solely to hide Mint defaults because "Mint does not natively let you remove any default categories or sub-categories"; broke after Mint's 2022 redesign. — [GitHub: mint.com-customize-default-categories](https://www.github.com/schrauger/mint.com-customize-default-categories)
- A blog example of Mint's two-level structure: "Food & Dining" with subcategories "Groceries", "Restaurants", "Coffee Shops". — [Techedvocate](https://www.thetechedvocate.org/?p=476194)

**Emma (UK)**
- Budgeting is two-tier: a Total budget ("what you can afford to spend every month"; computed either as income minus savings target, or recurring payments + expected variable spending) and optional Category budgets ("take your total budget, and divide the money between different areas of spending") shown against average spend; default categories can be replaced by Pro "Custom Categories". — [Emma blog: How to budget](https://emma-app.com/blog/2021/03/24/how-to-budget-using-the-emma-app/)
- "Committed spending" = "the payments Emma predicts will happen in your period for your budget", sourced only from Recurring payments; it reduces "left to spend". "An excluded upcoming payment is likely a credit card repayment or internal transfer" and should stay excluded. — [Emma help: Committed spending](https://help.emma-app.com/en/article/learn-committed-spending-1cg2t8v/)
- Emma later added "a new, dedicated Transfer category" so own-account moves are "a movement of funds, not an actual expense", plus per-category Exclude from Analytics/Budget, and Subcategories. — [Emma blog: Transfers, custom categories, exclusions](https://emma-app.com/blog/unlock-your-budgeting-power-introducing-new-transfers-custom-categories-exclusions)
- Emma staff: "your total budget in Emma tracks all your spending and committed spending, we don't have a way to exclude things so far". — [Emma community thread](https://community.emma-app.com/t/request-for-simpler-budgeting-feature/7359)

**Spendee**
- Entry types are Expenses, Income and Transfers; "Transfers are excluded from expense and income categories" but "counted in the Cash Flow"; a mis-linked transfer can be split back into income/expense. — [Spendee help: Transfers](https://help.spendee.com/article/234-transfers)
- Custom categories: user picks name, colour, icon. — [Spendee help: Custom category](https://help.spendee.com/article/227-how-to-create-a-custom-category)

**Lunch Money**
- Category groups are organisational only: "Only the categories nested inside a group can be assigned to transactions"; a group needs ≥1 category; a category cannot be converted to a group or vice versa. — [Lunch Money: Category groups](https://support.lunchmoney.app/setup/categories/category-groups)
- Category properties: "Treat as income" (feeds Income totals), "Exclude from budget" (left out of budgeting), "Exclude from totals" (not counted in spending or income; "useful for payments and/or transfers between accounts, or reimbursable items"); example category "Payment, Transfer" has both excludes on; sub-categories inherit from the group. — [Lunch Money: Category properties](https://support.lunchmoney.app/setup/categories/category-properties)
- Starter list "based on the average person's spending habits": Home, Auto / Groceries / Restaurants / Gas, Transportation / Entertainment / Personal Care / Shopping / Gifts / Travel / Bank Fees / Childcare / Pets, each with suggested subcategories (e.g., Home, Auto → Home maintenance, Car maintenance, Furniture & Appliances, Rent, Utilities, Phone & Internet, Insurance; Personal Care → Health insurance, Medical, Gym, Haircuts, Education). Advice: "Don't worry about having a perfect list of categories when starting out"; separate categories for income, withdrawals and transfers incl. credit-card payments that you "don't budget for". — [Lunch Money blog: Choosing categories](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-budget-categories)

**Actual Budget (open source, envelope/zero-sum)**
- Starts with "a few basic expense categories": Food (grocery + restaurant), Bills (same charge each month), Bills (Flexible) (bills that vary), Savings (saved / to move off-budget), General (everything else). Suggested (non-default) groups: "Crucial or really important", "Debt", "Daily expenses", "For a rainy day", "Savings goals". "it is easier to merge categories later than it is to manually move transactions to a new category." Transfers between on-budget accounts need no category; transfers to/from off-budget savings must be categorised; "all money entering or leaving your budget needs a category"; multiple income categories allowed. — [Actual docs: Starting fresh](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/starting-fresh)
- Categories are virtual envelopes; the system is zero-sum (every unit of income assigned). — [Actual docs: Envelope budgeting](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/envelope-budgeting)
- "Only one income group can ever exist and it cannot be deleted"; hidden categories "still impact your budget". — [Actual docs: Categories](https://actualbudget.org/docs/budgeting/categories/)

**50/30/20 (Warren & Tyagi, *All Your Worth*, 2005)**
- Origin: *All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan* (Simon & Schuster, 2005), Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi. — [Simon & Schuster book page](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/All-Your-Worth/Elizabeth-Warren/9780743279741)
- The "Balanced Money Formula": take-home pay split into Must-Haves (~50%, recurring commitments you must pay no matter what), Wants (~30%, discretionary) and Savings (≥20%, including debt paydown); Tyagi in 2005: "six months' worth of expenses in liquid savings". — [CNN Money, Aug 2005: Budget for real life](https://money.cnn.com/magazines/moneymag/moneymag_archive/2005/08/01/8267041/index.htm); [Get Rich Slowly: Balanced Money Formula](https://www.getrichslowly.org/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/346)
- Popular restatement: applies to after-tax income; Needs = rent/mortgage, utilities, groceries, healthcare, transportation, minimum debt payments; Wants = discretionary; 20% = savings + extra debt repayment. — [SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/50-30-20-budget/); [Ellevest](https://www.ellevest.com/magazine/personal-finance/50-30-20-rule)

**Envelope method (as implemented)**
- Of 45 top-rated free UK-store budgeting apps (2023 review), 33 monitor spend against budgets; of those, 7 use a single budget for all expenses and 26 use per-category "money envelope" budgets (4 of these premium-only). Only Goodbudget and SimpleBudget explicitly cite the envelope system in their store descriptions. 15 of 45 apps support subcategories (example: car → fines, fuel, parking, repairs). 35 of 45 support a Transfer transaction type. 38/45 ship default income categories (salary, bonus, gift, refund, business, interest); 40/45 ship default expense categories (grocery, shopping, transportation, bills, education, rent). — [Alenazi & Sas, "Evaluating Budgeting Apps: Limited Support for Budgeting Compared to Tracking", BCS HCI 2023 (PDF)](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/200745/1/Evaluating_Budgeting_Apps_Limited_Support_for_Budgeting_Compared_to_Tracking_.pdf)

### Inferences
- Two orthogonal axes exist in practice: (1) a **structural type** axis every app has (Income / Expense / Transfer / Excluded-Ignored), and (2) an optional **behavioural** axis (Fixed vs Flexible vs Non-monthly; Needs vs Wants vs Savings). Only Monarch and YNAB impose axis 2 by default; most apps leave merchant-style categories flat and let users build groups.
- The "about 60" Monarch defaults and the ~20-category Lunch Money/Rocket Money starter lists suggest the industry's steady state is ~15–25 leaf categories under ~5–12 groups, while method-driven products (YNAB, Actual, 50/30/20) push 3–5 top-level groups.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the exact current default leaf-category lists for Copilot, Rocket Money, Emma, Spendee, or Monarch (help pages 403'd / not indexed). Only partial names are confirmed above.
- Mint's full legacy list (Auto & Transport, Bills & Utilities, Food & Dining, Transfer, Hide from Budgets, etc.) could not be sourced from an accessible page; web.archive.org is not fetchable from this environment. Names in search snippets confirm "Food & Dining" and the two-level, undeletable structure only.
- YNAB's current default leaf categories inside each group were not listed on any fetched page.

## Key Question 2 — How "fixed / flexible / discretionary / savings / debt" are defined and where edge cases land

### Takeaway
The clearest operational definitions come from Monarch (Fixed = same amount every month and hard to reduce; Flexible = hardest to predict, easiest to control; Non-monthly = at least yearly but irregular; Goals = savings + aggressive debt payoff) and YNAB (Bills vs Frequent vs Non-Monthly "True Expenses" vs Goals). Car and insurance are split by cadence rather than by object: loan/lease and monthly premiums → Fixed; fuel/parking → Flexible; maintenance and annual/semiannual premiums → Non-monthly. Minimum debt payments → Fixed; extra payoff → Goals. Own-account transfers are a separate type excluded from spending everywhere.

### Cited Findings

**Definitions**
- Monarch Fixed: "Your fixed expenses are your most predictable, since you pay them each month" — rent/mortgage, monthly insurance premiums, "Minimums on your loans or other debts", subscriptions, utilities, phone, gym, childcare, automatic monthly donations. Non-Monthly: "You know they'll come at least once a year, but they either don't happen every month or vary widely from month to month" — taxes, gifts, car work, home repairs, travel, annual donations, annual subscriptions, annual/semiannual insurance; also called "sinking funds", "irregular" or "periodic expenses". Flexible: "These are the expenses that are hardest to predict, but easiest for you to control" — groceries, gas, medical copays, shopping, restaurants, entertainment, hobbies. Goals "are their own budget category and bucket" — emergency fund, retirement, 529, down payment, vacation, and aggressively paying off debt over 25% interest. Rationale: "Traditional budgets fail because they're too complicated to keep up with"; fixed and goals are "set and mostly forget"; "For everyday spending, you get one high-level number to track." — [Monarch blog: Fixed/flex spending](https://www.monarch.com/blog/fixed-flex-spending-what-it-is-and-how-to-get-started)
- Monarch help: Fixed bucket is for "predictable, recurring expenses that don't change much month to month and can't easily be reduced"; Non-Monthly budgets "will automatically split up an amount across the next 12 months" (snippets). — [Monarch help: Creating Your Budget](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048883631-Creating-Your-Budget-in-Monarch)
- Monarch's 23-category blog: "Fixed expenses are monthly bills that stay the same on a month-to-month basis"; "Think of flexible expenses as your everyday spending"; non-monthly "can easily become budget-busters if you don't plan for them". — [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget)
- YNAB: Bills = monthly expenses; Non-Monthly = True Expenses broken into monthly amounts; Goals = saving up; Quality of Life Goals = hobbies/health/entertainment (the "wants" analogue). — [YNAB blog](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-many-ynab-categories)
- Actual's defaults separate "Bills" (same charge monthly) from "Bills (Flexible)" (varies month to month). — [Actual docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/starting-fresh)
- Emma "committed spending" = predicted recurring payments for the period; functionally the fixed/recurring layer. — [Emma help](https://help.emma-app.com/en/article/learn-committed-spending-1cg2t8v/)
- Copilot "Recurrings" track "expected monthly spend, such as bills, subscriptions, and reimbursements" and are excluded from the dashboard spending chart. — [Copilot quick start](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/11157550-quick-start-guide)

**Edge-case placement table (sourced)**
- Car loan/lease + monthly car insurance → Monarch "Cars" (Fixed); fuel, parking, tolls → "Transportation" (Flexible); maintenance → "Home and car maintenance" (Non-monthly). — [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget)
- Car payments + car insurance + public transport → Rocket Money "Auto And Transport"; health insurance → "Health And Wellness"; no standalone insurance category. — [Rocket Money Learn](https://rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/budget-categories)
- Car insurance premiums → YNAB "Non-Monthly" (True Expense). — [YNAB blog](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-many-ynab-categories)
- Lunch Money starter: Car maintenance and Insurance sit under "Home, Auto"; Gasoline/Parking/Tolls under "Gas, Transportation"; Health insurance under "Personal Care". — [Lunch Money blog](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-budget-categories)
- Installment/debt minimums → Monarch Fixed ("Minimums on your loans"); extra payoff → Goals ("Debt Repayment – …"). Rocket Money → "Loan Payment" category (10–20%). — [Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/blog/fixed-flex-spending-what-it-is-and-how-to-get-started); [Rocket Money](https://rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/budget-categories)
- Credit-card payments: YNAB treats them as transfers to the card account that land in the "Credit Card Payment" category, funded automatically when budgeted money is spent on the card; categorising a card payment as an expense double-counts (snippets). Tiller: categorise both sides as a Transfer-type "Credit Card Payment"; "treat them as an Expense instead" only if you want debt payments in the budget. — [YNAB support: Imported credit card payments](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-handle-imported-credit-card-payments-SkXcoodci.md); [Tiller: Understanding transfers](https://intercom.help/tiller/en/articles/432687-understanding-transfers)
- Transfers between own accounts: excluded from spend in Copilot ("don't count toward your monthly spend"), Spendee (excluded from expense/income, in cash flow), Rocket Money ("Transfers" non-spending category), Emma (dedicated Transfer category), Tiller (Transfer type "excluded from budget dashboards and cash flow calculations", both sides "sum to $0"), Lunch Money ("Exclude from totals"), Actual (no category between on-budget accounts; categorised if to off-budget savings). — sources as cited above per app.
- Savings & investments: Actual "Savings" is an expense category for money moved off-budget; Monarch puts emergency fund / retirement / investments in Goals; YNAB puts them in Goals categories with targets; 50/30/20 puts them in the 20% bucket with debt paydown. — [Actual](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/starting-fresh); [Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget); [YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-to-embrace-your-true-expenses); [SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/50-30-20-budget/)
- Taxes: Monarch Non-monthly ("Property taxes", "Taxes that are due quarterly or annually"); Rocket Money has a "Tax-Deductible" non-spending tag, not a tax-expense group. — [Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget); [Rocket Money](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/10770105-understanding-non-spending-categories-in-your-budget)
- Travel / big one-offs / gifts: Monarch Non-monthly ("Travel and vacations", "Gifts and entertaining", "Clothing or home accessories"); YNAB Goals (travel) or Non-Monthly (holiday gifts); Lunch Money "Travel" group with Flights/Accommodation/Food/Attractions subcategories. — [Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/the-23-budget-categories-you-need-in-your-budget); [YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/blog/how-many-ynab-categories); [Lunch Money](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-budget-categories)

### Inferences
- The strongest common denominator across methods is *cadence*, not *object*: monthly-and-stable → Fixed/Bills; monthly-but-variable → Flexible/Frequent; less-than-monthly → Non-monthly/True Expenses/sinking fund; future-oriented → Goals. A car therefore legitimately spans three groups, and apps that force one "Auto" bucket (Rocket Money, Lunch Money starter) trade behavioural clarity for merchant-mapping simplicity.
- "Discretionary/fun" is not a first-class group anywhere by default; it is approximated by YNAB's "Quality of Life Goals", Monarch's Flexible bucket (minus groceries/gas/medical), Rocket Money's "Dining And Drinks" + "Recreation And Entertainment", and 50/30/20's Wants.

### Gaps
- No app documentation found that specifies where *installment plans on a credit card* (common in Israel: "tashlumim") should go; US apps treat the card statement as a transfer problem, not an installment problem.
- No official Monarch guidance found on whether "Non-monthly" amounts roll over if unspent (help page 403).

## Key Question 3 — What behavioural research says about number of categories, a "fun" bucket, mental accounting, and fixed-vs-variable framing

### Takeaway
People budget by category almost universally (≈90% of budgeters) and spontaneously form about five top-level groups (mode = 5, range 3–18) that map onto "bills, groceries, household products, clothing, entertainment", organised along two dimensions — typical spend and necessity. Field evidence that *more* categories improve outcomes is absent or negative: a 9,035-user RCT found one-number and category budgets produced identical spending; splitting a budget into multiple small budgets increased total spending in one research programme; making categories salient *increased* a tempting purchase in a 400-person lab study; and exhausting a narrow budget ("bottom dollar") reduces satisfaction. No rigorous study was found that directly tests a "fun money" bucket's effect on adherence, nor one that tests showing fixed vs variable separately.

### Cited Findings

**Mental accounting foundations**
- Thaler (1999): mental accounting is "the set of cognitive operations used by individuals and households to organize, evaluate, and keep track of financial activities"; "Expenditures are grouped into categories (housing, food, etc.) and spending is sometimes constrained by implicit or explicit budgets"; accounts "can be balanced daily, weekly, yearly, and so on, and can be defined narrowly or broadly"; each component violates fungibility. — [Thaler, "Mental Accounting Matters", J. Behav. Decis. Making 12(3) 1999 (PDF)](https://people.bath.ac.uk/mnsrf/Teaching%202011/Thaler-99.pdf)
- Heath & Soll (1996): consumers budget by expense category, and category budgets can cause *under*-consumption of goods whose budget is exhausted; effects strongest for purchases typical of their category; three studies controlling for satiation and income. — [Heath & Soll, J. Consumer Research 23(1) 40–52](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v23y1996i1p40-52.html)

**How people actually budget (descriptive)**
- Survey of 3,800 US consumers + 2019 data on ~200,000 Commonwealth Bank of Australia customers: about two-thirds were budgeting; of budgeters "90 percent also reported tracking their spending within distinct budgetary categories"; granularity varies — some "simply separated bills from discretionary spending", others kept line items for internet, water, car payment, electricity; "Eighty-five percent of respondents said they would adjust their spending" after overspending a category, but "less than 30 percent" would after underspending; authors say "a lot of the work in mental accounting presupposes budgets without a strong descriptive understanding". — [Chicago Booth Review on Zhang, Sussman, Wang-Ly & Lyu, "How Consumers Budget", JEBO 2022](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/2023/february/five-insights-about-budgeting)
- Fei, Bartels & Zhang (2024): people represent expenditures as a hierarchical taxonomy, not one flat level. In a pile-sort of 64 expenditures, "five was the mode of the number of groups that participants constructed on the initial-sort (range = 3-18)"; the five consensus groups are "bills (e.g., rent), groceries (e.g., juice), household products (e.g., shampoo), clothing items (e.g., shirts), and entertainment (e.g., dining out, airplane tickets)"; the two underlying dimensions "roughly correspond to 1) the typical spending on the item and 2) the necessity of spending on the item". "some consumers generated three initial level groups while others generated more than a dozen", yet consensus on relative distances held. When people overspend on an item "they were more likely to spontaneously adjust spending for items closer in representation than those further away", shown in lab studies and field data of "6.5 million grocery shopping trips over twelve years". — [Fei, Bartels & Zhang, BFI Working Paper 2024-159 (PDF)](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/BFI_WP_2024-159.pdf)
- A Cognitive Science Society paper (Katz & Sussman) reports that resource monitoring "is positively related to the number of categories they form" (search snippet; paper not fetched). — [CogSci proceedings PDF](https://journalpub.escholarship.org/cognitivesciencesociety/article/29707/galley/19564/download)

**Does category granularity change outcomes? (causal)**
- Clarity Money × Irrational Labs × Common Cents Lab field RCT (Android, from 30 Sept 2019, 13 weeks, N = 9,035): Informational Control (N=4,368) vs One-Number weekly budget (N=2,723) vs Category Budgeting (overall number + category goals, N=1,944). Both budget variants raised engagement "from once every 4 weeks to once every 3 weeks" (p<0.001) but "in all conditions, engagement declined over time". Average spending: control $675.97, single budget $681.08, category budget $673.25 (all p>0.4); no difference in spending variability; budgeters spent "1.3-1.4 times what they intended"; spending in *budgeted* categories was about $30 higher than in non-budgeted ones (p<.001) controlling for usual spend. Verdict: "It didn't work." — [Common Cents Lab case study](https://finsights.guide/case-studies/GfCI1P8I/clarity-money-budgeting-experiment)
- Common Cents Lab lab study (N≈400): showing an expense summary with more salient budget categories (intended to make the opportunity cost of ordering takeout visible when groceries were already at home) *backfired* — treatment participants were significantly *more* likely to spend on the temptation; authors suggest salient categories acted as a "counterproductive reminder" that other categories can be adjusted. — [Common Cents Lab: Budget categories & opportunity costs](https://finsights.guide/case-studies/t8a0U930/exploring-how-budget-categories-and-opportunity-co)
- Pocheptsova Ghosh & Huang ("Dynamic Budget Monitoring"): showing the remaining-budget amount increases certainty that money is left and *raises* spending, especially late in the period; interventions that made budget information less precise or shortened the window attenuated this, but "breaking a big budget into multiple small budgets, increased total spending" (secondary summary of the authors' technical report; press coverage reported increases "by up to a third"). — [CEPR working paper PDF](https://cepr.org/system/files/2022-08/Dynamic%20Budget%20Monitoring%20-%20When%20Access%20to%20Budget%20Feedback%20Leads%20to%20Increase%20in%20Spending%20-%20Anastasiya%20Pocheptsova%20Ghosh%20&%20Liang%20Huang.pdf); [BehavioralEconomics.com project page](https://behavioraleconomics.com/the-budgeting-app-trap-when-spending-information-backfires); [Fintech Times coverage](https://thefintechtimes.com/research-finds-budgeting-apps-increase-spending-by-up-to-a-third/)
- Sussman & Alter (2012, 7 studies): people forecast ordinary expenses well but "spent almost double as much as they expected to on exceptional expenses"; cause is narrow categorisation — each exceptional expense is construed as unique; the fix is to consider exceptional items "as part of a larger set of purchases" (i.e., a pooled irregular bucket). — [NYU Stern research highlight](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/adam-alter-exceptional-expense); [JCR record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-665833.html)
- Soster, Gershoff & Bearden (2014, 6 studies): "bottom dollar effect" — spending that exhausts a budget decreases satisfaction with the purchase relative to spending when resources remain; stronger the harder the budget was earned, weaker when replenishment is near or a windfall arrives; mediated by pain of payment. — [JCR 41(3) 656–677](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/677223); [U. Arkansas press release](https://news.uark.edu/articles/24384/bottom-dollar-effect-influences-consumer-satisfaction-with-products-study-finds)

**Regulator / HCI findings**
- CFPB (2017, with ideas42): "consumers aspire to manage their spending but for many reasons, many consumers spend more than intended"; "Budgeting and tracking spending are often considered to be overwhelming or too much of a hassle, and even those consumers who have a budget generally do not benchmark their spending to their budget frequently or regularly"; consumers "may treat many purchases as special and off-budget"; over 90% of interviewees wanted a real-time point-of-sale budget-feedback tool; cites a lab/field study in which composite spending feedback on card receipts cut spending 9.6%. — [CFPB, Consumer Insights on Managing Spending (PDF)](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/201702_cfpb_Consumer-Insights-on-Managing-Spending.pdf)
- Alenazi & Sas (2023): of 45 apps, 7 use a single budget and 26 per-category envelopes; the authors argue "spending money from a general pot rather than specific pots for different expense categories, can more easily lead to over expenditure" and recommend grouping "into categories and subcategories" that can be "predefined by the system or defined by the user"; they also flag "limited clarity regarding the two important terms of accounts and transactions". — [BCS HCI 2023 PDF](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/200745/1/Evaluating_Budgeting_Apps_Limited_Support_for_Budgeting_Compared_to_Tracking_.pdf); extended in [Alenazi & Sas, Interacting with Computers 37(5), 2025](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/222780)
- Snow & Vyas (CHI EA 2015, 15 households): "a misalignment between people's actual budgeting practices and those supported by off-the-shelf budgeting aids". — [Southampton ePrints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/386438)

### Inferences
- The behavioural literature supports **few, broad, cadence/necessity-based groups** (≈5) as the natural mental unit, with finer leaf categories kept for *attribution* rather than *limits*. Setting limits on many narrow leaves risks (a) bottom-dollar dissatisfaction, (b) Heath–Soll under-consumption in exhausted categories alongside overspending in others, (c) the "multiple small budgets → more total spending" effect, and (d) no measured spending benefit (Clarity Money).
- Sussman & Alter's result is the strongest empirical argument for a **pooled Non-monthly / irregular bucket** (YNAB True Expenses, Monarch Non-monthly): it directly counters the ~2× underestimation of exceptional expenses.
- A **discretionary/"fun" bucket** is defensible on theory (Fei et al.'s "necessity" dimension; Zhang et al.'s "bills vs discretionary" split being the most common minimal taxonomy; bottom-dollar effect favouring one larger entertainment pot over several small ones) but is not directly validated by any experiment found.
- Showing **fixed vs variable separately** is not tested as such; the closest evidence (Monarch's design rationale, Emma's "committed spending", CFPB's "hard to know how much money is left") supports it as a way to compute a single honest "left to spend" number, which is where the feedback research (Pocheptsova Ghosh & Huang; CFPB/Poddar) locates the behavioural lever — with the caveat that a precise remaining balance can itself license spending.

### Gaps
- No study found that directly manipulates the *number of top-level groups* (e.g., 3 vs 5 vs 10) and measures adherence or satisfaction.
- No peer-reviewed test found of a "fun money"/allowance bucket on adherence or satisfaction; one popular article claimed a *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 5%-allowance finding but gave no citation and could not be verified — treat as unsupported.
- No study found that compares a fixed/variable split view against a flat category view.
- Katz & Sussman poster and the Pocheptsova Ghosh & Huang full paper were not readable (connection refused / PDF); figures for the "multiple budgets" effect size are unconfirmed.

## Key Question 4 — Person-to-person payments (Venmo/Bit/PayBox) and refunds in category totals

### Takeaway
Apps distinguish *own-account transfers* (always excluded from spend) from *P2P payments to other people* (treated as spend or income, often mis-detected as transfers). For refunds and reimbursements the dominant documented pattern is **netting against the original spending category** (YNAB, Monarch users), with a minority pattern of a **dedicated offsetting Transfer-type "Reimbursement/Refund" category** (Tiller) or a **non-spending "Reimbursements" tag** (Rocket Money). No app documentation was found for Israeli Bit/PayBox specifically.

### Cited Findings
- YNAB: ask "Is this inflow offsetting an expense that's already in YNAB?" — if no, it is income (Ready to Assign); if yes, "categorize it back to the original spending category" so income vs expense stays accurate; "a reimbursement from a friend" is listed as an exception to Ready to Assign; credit-card refunds go "to the original spending category, not Ready to Assign". — [YNAB support: Income and other inflows](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/how-to-add-income-and-other-inflows-H1ZNjfZJi.md); [YNAB support: Credit card refunds](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/credit-card-refunds-and-returns-H1J7qDWkj.md)
- Tiller: create a Transfer-type "Reimbursement" category and apply it to both the reimbursable purchase and the incoming deposit so they offset; same for "Refund"; Transfer-type categories are "excluded from budget dashboards and cash flow calculations". — [Tiller: Understanding transfers](https://intercom.help/tiller/en/articles/432687-understanding-transfers)
- Rocket Money: "Reimbursements" is a non-spending category for "receiving money or getting paid for money that you've spent"; "Transfers" and "Ignored" are separate non-spending categories. — [Rocket Money help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/10770105-understanding-non-spending-categories-in-your-budget)
- Copilot: internal transfers cannot be categorised and don't count toward monthly spend; to categorise one, change its type to "Regular"; a reviewer notes Venmo is ingested via "email forwarding for Venmo receipts within the Copilot app", and that shared costs inflate category estimates ("would guess double my actual out-of-pocket cost"). — [Copilot Categories FAQ](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/10216528-categories-faq); [Money with Katie review](https://moneywithkatie.com/copilot-review-a-budgeting-app-that-finally-gets-it-right/)
- Lunch Money: "Exclude from totals" is "useful for payments and/or transfers between accounts, or reimbursable items". — [Lunch Money: Category properties](https://support.lunchmoney.app/setup/categories/category-properties)
- Spendee: transfers out of expense/income totals but in cash flow; a wrongly linked transfer can be converted back to income/expense. — [Spendee help](https://help.spendee.com/article/234-transfers)
- Emma: dedicated Transfer category; excluded upcoming payments are typically "a credit card repayment or internal transfer". — [Emma blog](https://emma-app.com/blog/unlock-your-budgeting-power-introducing-new-transfers-custom-categories-exclusions); [Emma help](https://help.emma-app.com/en/article/learn-committed-spending-1cg2t8v/)
- Practitioner guidance: sending money to a friend for dinner "is an expense, not a transfer"; bank-synced P2P payments often arrive classified as transfers and need manual review; payments from a Venmo *balance* never hit the bank feed and must be logged separately; a Monarch user nets friend repayments and merchant refunds into the original category because "reimbursements and returning something is not income". — [Finny blog: Venmo/Zelle in your budget](https://getfinny.app/blog/track-venmo-zelle-payments-budget); [r/MonarchMoney thread](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/MonarchMoney/comments/1ia0mfg/categorizing_returnsrefunds_and_reimbursements); [Simplifi community: Zelle auto-classified as transfer](https://community.simplifimoney.com/discussion/15053/why-are-zelle-payments-automatically-classified-as-transfers-when-they-are-payments)
- Sussman & Alter and the Rocket Money/Tiller designs both imply the same data-model requirement: inflows need a flag "offsets prior spend" vs "new income". — [NYU Stern](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/adam-alter-exceptional-expense); [Tiller](https://intercom.help/tiller/en/articles/432687-understanding-transfers)

### Inferences
- Three distinct P2P cases must be modelled: (1) own-account transfer → excluded type; (2) payment *to* another person for goods/share of bill → ordinary expense in the relevant category; (3) repayment *from* another person → negative spend in the original category (preferred) or a Reimbursement offset type. Treating (3) as income inflates both income and spend.
- "Net into original category" keeps category totals honest for budgeting; the "offset category" approach keeps the gross spend visible for shared-expense tracking. Apps pick one; some (Rocket Money) expose a tag so the user can choose.

### Gaps
- No documentation found from Monarch's help centre on Venmo/Zelle (403); findings are community-sourced.
- Nothing found on Bit / PayBox / Israeli bank-feed classification of P2P transfers; the Venmo/Zelle patterns are assumed to transfer.

## Key Question 5 — Group-level vs category-level targets, and remapping

### Takeaway
Three postures exist: (a) **category-only targets** (YNAB targets, Actual goal templates, Emma category budgets, Rocket Money, Spendee); (b) **both levels, user-chosen** (Monarch "Group Budgeting" and Lunch Money "Enable Category Group Budgeting", Copilot "Unassigned Budget" per group); (c) **one number for all flexible spend** (Monarch Flex default, Emma Total budget, the Clarity Money "one-number" arm). Vendors justify group targets with flexibility and reduced upkeep ("without getting stuck in the nitty gritty"); the only RCT found shows one-number and category budgets perform identically on spending.

### Cited Findings
- Monarch Group-level budgeting: "set and track budgets at the category group level instead of per category", e.g. one "Food & Dining" target instead of separate Groceries/Restaurants/Coffee; purpose: "set some guardrails around your spending, without getting stuck in the nitty gritty"; "Group level budgeting does not impact cash flow reporting". Group budgets "can be applied to all groups, a few, or just one" (snippet). — [Monarch: Group-level budgeting](https://www.monarch.com/group-level-budgeting); [Monarch help: Group Budgeting](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/18345219809940-Group-Budgeting)
- Monarch Flex: a single "flex number" (income minus Fixed, Non-Monthly and Goals) governs all flexible spending; optional limits on individual categories inside the bucket; Monarch "alerts you when a category goes over its limit" in category mode. — [Monarch: Flex vs category](https://www.monarch.com/flex-vs-category-budgeting-how-to-choose-whats-right-for-you)
- Copilot: budgets per subcategory *and* an "Unassigned Budget" per group ("Set an Unassigned Budget if you do not want to set a budget for each subcategory"; also to absorb subcategory overages); example Food group $500 instead of $250 + $250 because spend "shifts between cooking and dining out". Rebalancing tool reallocates category budgets "without changing the total amount spent". — [Copilot help: Groups](https://intercom.help/copilotmoney/en/articles/3767655-groups-of-categories); [Money with Katie](https://moneywithkatie.com/copilot-review-a-budgeting-app-that-finally-gets-it-right/)
- Lunch Money (Budgeting v2): toggle "Enable Category Group Budgeting" per group; then budgets can be set at group *and* subcategory level; rollover settings for group-budgeted categories live at the group level; per-category rollover to same category or to general pool (snippets). — [Lunch Money: Migrating to Budgeting v2](https://support.lunchmoney.app/migrating-to-budgeting-v2); [Lunch Money: Step 2 setting up your budget](https://support.lunchmoney.app/guides/budgeting/step-2-setting-up-your-budget)
- YNAB: a target is added to a category (amount, timeframe, or both); no group-level target documented. — [YNAB support: Getting started with Targets](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/getting-started-with-targets-ryAEP08xC)
- Actual: goals/templates are per category (status shown on the category balance); the only group action is "Overwrite with templates", which fills every category in the group. Template types: Simple, By, Periodic, Percent, Schedule, Average, Copy, Remainder. — [Actual docs: Goal templates](https://actualbudget.org/docs/experimental/goal-templates)
- Emma: Total budget first, Category budgets optional. — [Emma blog](https://emma-app.com/blog/2021/03/24/how-to-budget-using-the-emma-app/)
- Rocket Money: budgets are per category; users ask for "a simple total budget" and for fixed/discretionary segments and subcategories. — [Rocket Money help: Creating a budget](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/2649810-creating-a-budget); [Canny request](https://rocketmoney.canny.io/feature-requests/p/subcategories)
- Evidence: one-number vs category budgets — no spending difference; both raised engagement equally. — [Common Cents Lab](https://finsights.guide/case-studies/GfCI1P8I/clarity-money-budgeting-experiment)

**Remapping / editing categories**
- YNAB: payee-level default category with "Always use my choice"; recategorising old transactions never changes the default. — [YNAB support](https://support.ynab.com/en_us/categorizing-transactions-a-guide-HyRl60sks.md)
- Monarch: defaults can be disabled, renamed, not deleted; rules "rename and recategorize your transactions as they come in". — [Monarch help: Default Categories](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048883851); [Monarch help: Transaction rules](https://help.monarch.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048393372)
- Copilot: "Other" undeletable; Regular vs Excluded flag per category; Copilot Intelligence "surfaces the top two guesses" on correction. — [Copilot FAQ](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/10216528-categories-faq); [Copilot: Copilot Intelligence](https://intercom.help/copilotmoney/en/articles/8182433-copilot-intelligence-for-spending)
- Lunch Money: archiving a group archives its children; properties inherited from group; category↔group not convertible. — [Lunch Money: Editing categories](https://support.lunchmoney.app/setup/categories/editing-categories); [Category groups](https://support.lunchmoney.app/setup/categories/category-groups)
- Actual: merging a category moves its budget and balance into the target; hide rather than delete. — [Actual docs: Categories](https://actualbudget.org/docs/budgeting/categories/)
- Rocket Money: custom categories Premium-only; each assigned to Expenses/Earnings/Ignored. — [Rocket Money help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/3332081-reviewing-editing-and-creating-transaction-categories)
- Mint (legacy): defaults undeletable; custom subcategories only under Mint's main categories. — [Mint help](https://help.mint.com/Accounts-and-Transactions/888960541/How-do-I-add-a-transaction-subcategory.htm)

### Inferences
- The industry trend (Monarch 2023+, Lunch Money v2, Copilot) is toward **optional group-level targets layered over leaf categories used for attribution**, which matches the research picture (broad groups as the behavioural unit, leaves for reporting).
- Every app keeps a small set of **protected/system categories** (Other/Uncategorized, Transfer, Income, Credit Card Payment, Ready to Assign) that cannot be deleted and sit outside spend totals; a custom taxonomy should reserve these as types, not as ordinary categories.

### Gaps
- No vendor design post found that *explains* why YNAB and Actual avoid group targets; only the absence is documented.
- Monarch help pages on flex rollover and category-within-flex limits could not be fetched (403); behaviour of unspent group budget rollover in Monarch is unconfirmed.
